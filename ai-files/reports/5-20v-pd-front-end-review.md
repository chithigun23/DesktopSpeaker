# 5–20 V USB-PD front-end review

Review date: 2026-10-05. Scope: independent design review only; no schematic or library changes. Assume USB-PD SPR operation through 20 V. The STUSB4500 is limited to fixed PDOs through 20 V; this design is not a 28–48 V EPR input. The original review was against the then-active BQ25895 schematic. `plan.md` now selects BQ25792RQMR, a native 3.6–24 V 1S buck-boost charger; the earlier proposed pre-buck is superseded. Until that replacement is integrated, the active BQ25895 sheet still makes 20 V unsafe.

## Findings

- **STUSB4500 can autonomously request three fixed PDOs**, with PDO1 fixed at 5 V and PDO2/3 programmable from 5–20 V in 50 mV steps. A 5/9/20 V NVM profile is supported; 5/15/20 V is another choice if the system can use 15 V. It has no PPS sink profile. It selects the highest-priority exact voltage/current match; with no match it establishes a 5 V contract. Program, read back, and document the chosen NVM profile. [STUSB4500 datasheet, DS12499](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/stusb4500.pdf)
- **A 5 V fallback does not entitle this product to 2 A.** The source's advertised Type-C current may be default, 1.5 A, or 3 A. STUSB4500's datasheet offers optional `POWER_OK2/3` GPIO encoding for those three classes (NVM configuration 3); the currently captured default configuration uses those pins for PD contract status, so don't assume firmware can read Rp without an intentional pin/configuration change. On PD mismatch it requests source 5 V current with PDO1's sink current as its maximum. Keep charger/system current within the actual contract/permission. A “5 V / 2 A” adapter label is not generic USB permission. See STUSB4500 datasheet sections 2.2.8/Table 4, 3.3.2–3.3.3.
- **The active 5/9 V protections conflict with a 20 V contract.** U18 TPS26600's present ~11 V OVP disconnects a valid 20 V rail. D8 SMA6J10A has 10 V standoff and starts breakdown at 11.1 V (12.3 V max); it will conduct/clamp during 20 V operation. D9's 12 V zener also clamps the STUSB4500 VBUS sense node in normal 20 V operation. These are not compatible with 20 V SPR as currently connected.
- **Historical charger constraint (superseded design choice):** active `VBUS_PD` currently feeds BQ25895 directly, whose input ceiling is 14 V, so the present schematic cannot accept 20 V. The reviewed replacement is BQ25792RQMR (native 3.6–24 V input, 1S buck-boost); with that integration no additional pre-buck is planned. Keep 20 V behind the coordinated protected PD rail and away from 5 V-only audio electronics. BQ25792 integration and its current policy remain unfinished.
- **STUSB4500 voltage limits:** VDD recommended operating range ends at 22 V; VBUS pins are specified for 28 V high-voltage capability/absolute maximum. The often-mentioned 22 V value is also the CC-pin short-to-VBUS protection level; it is not the VBUS sense-pin maximum. Normal 20 V is within the monitor-pin limit, but raw D6 TVS2200's 28.4 V maximum clamp is already above 28 V before trace overshoot. Protect/limit VBUS_VS_DISCH transients below the actual pin limit; do not use the present 12 V clamp for a 20 V rail. Keep U19's raw-VBUS-fed 5 V LDO concept; TPS7B84-Q1 accepts up to 40 V (42 V abs max). Its startup/dropout and surge path still need qualification.
- **Q1/Q2:** STL9P3LLH6 are 30 V P-channel devices. 20 V steady state is nominally below VDS rating, but leaves only 10 V to rating and little margin against the TVS2200's 28.4 V clamp plus wiring overshoot. Validate VDS/VGS waveforms, or replace this pair with a higher-voltage/lower-loss path. The existing TPS26600 itself is a 4.2–60 V eFuse, so 20 V operation is within its input rating once UV/OV/ILIM settings are redesigned; it is currently preceded by the 30 V PMOS pair.

## Integration recommendation

1. **Select the PD controller after comparing its current-status/startup behavior with BQ25792.** Set and verify a fixed SPR profile/voltage range only after the protection path and load budget are defined. Keep PDO current values within guaranteed usable input power, not merely source maximums. Add no EPR request.
2. **Use the protected PD rail directly as BQ25792 input; no extra pre-buck is planned.** Keep it separate from 5 V-only audio electronics. The active schematic remains BQ25895-based and must retain its 5/9 V-only profile until the BQ25792 replacement and protection redesign are integrated.
3. **Redesign coordinated protection thresholds.** Raise U18 OVP above the maximum valid negotiated 20 V plus its tolerance/ripple, while below the safe limit of every downstream part; retain an independent fault cutoff. Remove/replace D8 with a surge clamp whose standoff exceeds maximum normal 20 V and whose actual clamp is below the protected rail's limits. Remove/retune D9 so it is off at 20 V; use a series-limited monitor clamp above normal 20 V and below the ST pin limit, with transient-current and tolerance checks. Check D6's 28.4 V clamp versus the ST 28 V pin maximum and all upstream parts; changing OVP alone cannot fix raw-node transient stress.
4. **Choose the series switch strategy against loss and transients.** Incremental option: retain TPS26600, recalculating OVP/UVLO/ILIM and verify its power/thermal behavior at 5 V load, while replacing or proving the 30 V PMOS pair for the D6 clamp plus layout overshoot. Lower-loss alternative: LTC4368-2 plus back-to-back 60 V N-FETs and shunt. The candidate's 20 mΩ shunt gives nominal ~2.5 A breaker at 50 mV; its published 40–60 mV threshold implies roughly 1.98–3.03 A around normal output before resistor corners. This is a breaker, not a regulated current limit. It needs gate logic polarity adaptation and inrush/OV transient validation. It reduces series loss versus the existing 250 mΩ eFuse allowance plus ~45 mΩ paired-PMOS estimate, but is a larger redesign. See `LTC4368-2-protection-redesign-candidate.md` and ADI [LTC4368 datasheet](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/ltc4368.pdf).
5. **Validate before hardware:** worst-case PDO/NVM negotiation including 5 V mismatch/fallback; 5→9→20 V transitions; eFuse disconnect/retry timing; inrush and stored energy; raw/sensed/protected rail overshoot; zener/TVS pulse energy; switch SOA/thermal; BQ25792 input/output and source-current policy. Capture peak voltages at the controller VBUS monitor, switch path, converter/charger input and battery/system nodes. Existing reports are schematic-level only; no hardware transient qualification is recorded.

## Negotiation breadth: all common SPR fixed voltages

The earlier “5/9/20” profile is only three simultaneous fixed sink PDOs, not universal coverage. STUSB4500 hard-fixes PDO1 at 5 V and allows only two more fixed PDOs at a time; it cannot simultaneously match 5/9/12/15/20 V. This matters because ST compares exact voltage and sufficient current, then stops at its first matching sink PDO. A source offering only 12 V or 15 V can therefore fall back to 5 V if that PDO is absent from the active profile.

**Recommendation: keep STUSB4500 and add MCU-managed dynamic profile selection** if using multiple common adapters is a real requirement. This keeps the current autonomous/dead-battery 5 V startup and safe NVM fallback, while the planned STM32G031 can broaden matching after boot. ST's own UM2650 documents writing PDOs into runtime registers (PDO1 fixed at 5 V; PDO2/3 mutable) and sending a USB-PD Soft_Reset message to trigger a new negotiation. ST's STSW-STUSB003/UM2473 example specifically supports source-PDO access, changing sink PDOs and aligning sink profiles to source profiles; its example target is an STM32F072RB, so the driver/policy needs porting and timing validation on the planned G031. Source capability data must be captured promptly from the ST controller's message/register path. [ST UM2650 programming guide](https://www.st.com/resource/en/user_manual/um2650-the-stusb4500-software-programing-guide-stmicroelectronics.pdf), [ST dynamic-input-power manual UM2473](https://www.st.com/resource/en/user_manual/um2473-managing-the-dynamic-input-power-with-the-stusb4500-and-the-stm32f072rb-stmicroelectronics.pdf), [STSW-STUSB003 overview](https://www.st.com/en/embedded-software/stsw-stusb003.html)

Suggested policy: NVM boots with 5/9/20 V limits and PDO1 set to a conservative 5 V current; once the MCU is powered, read the source's fixed PDO list, filter by the front-end/BQ25792 qualified voltage/current/power limits, then write PDO2/3 to include the best available exact fixed voltages (5 V remains PDO1). If the best useful choice is 12 V, offer 12 V in PDO2/3; for a 15 V-only source, include 15 V. Issue the documented Soft_Reset message and confirm PS_RDY/contract voltage before raising load or charger demand. Recompute when source capabilities change or an attach/detach occurs. The active BQ25895 schematic still blocks 20 V; after planned BQ25792 integration no separate buck is required. Do not write NVM for every attachment.

The STUSB4500 datasheet documents optional `PWR_OK_CFG=11` behavior that reports non-PD Type-C advertised-current classes on `POWER_OK2` and `POWER_OK3` pins (default/1.5 A/3 A). This is a GPIO mode, not a verified I²C status register, and it replaces the default pins' PD-contract meaning; the active schematic/firmware must route and configure it before claiming MCU source-current feedback. UM2473/STSW-STUSB003 source code appears to provide a software status path, but exact exposed I²C fields are not established here. Keep this interface unresolved until the integration owner confirms whether to use the GPIO pair or a verified software path. PD sources initially present 5 V so the controller and MCU can start; that initial 5 V does **not** mean 2 A is available. An Rp class indicates Type-C source capability, not USB host enumeration permission: the current PCM2902C descriptor is 100 mA, so apply the planned conservative 100 mA attach budget before enumeration/configuration and define suspend current separately. A USB-A adapter/cable without valid detection or contract must use its applicable conservative policy, not an assumed 2 A.

### Alternatives reviewed

- **TI TPS25730:** simpler autonomous controller/power-path alternative; the later review below makes it the preferred candidate for fixed 5–20 V SPR if conservative non-PD fallback is acceptable. TI documents resistor-strapped minimum/maximum sink voltage configurations spanning 5, 9, 12, 15 and 20 V, plus operating/maximum current configuration and integrated path. It starts from dead battery and accepts common fixed SPR profiles without the STUSB4500's three-profile MCU selection. It still requires MCU/I²C policy if the charger current must be set from the accepted contract. TI [TPS25730 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps25730.pdf), especially sections 8.3.11–8.3.14 and 9.2.
- **TI TPS25751:** not a simpler substitute for this sink-only port. TI requires an external EEPROM/application configuration bundle for normal operation (or a host that supplies it) and targets more configurable sink/source system architectures. It does support dead-battery Rd and VBUS-powered startup, but that configuration burden does not solve a requirement that STUSB4500 plus a small MCU cannot address. [TPS25751 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps25751.pdf), sections 8.3.2, 8.4 and 9.

Initial recommendation above to keep STUSB4500 is superseded by the later TPS25730D architecture and I²C review below. Do not claim full 5–20 V common-PDO support from a static three-PDO STUSB4500 NVM profile.

## TPS25730D integrated sink path and architecture trade

TPS25730D deserves a full architecture comparison rather than treating the captured ST chain as fixed. TI specifies its integrated PPHV switch at **16 mΩ typ / 19 mΩ max at 25°C and 6.5 A**, rising to **24/29 mΩ at 125°C** and **27/32 mΩ at 150°C**. At 2 A, using datasheet maxima, this is about 76 mV / 76 mW at 25°C and 116 mV / 116 mW at 125°C. It integrates back-to-back sink-path switching, slew control, reverse-current protection and programmable VBUS OVP. That is far below the existing TPS26600 250 mΩ maximum allowance plus about 45 mΩ paired-PMOS estimate (about 1.18 W at 2 A using those independent figures). The TPS25730D is a lower-loss and materially simpler path than discrete LTC4368-2 + dual N-FET + 20 mΩ shunt too: the existing candidate estimates about 23 mΩ at room temperature; TPS25730D is 19 mΩ maximum at 25°C before thermal-layout checks. It is not a current-regulating eFuse: TI specifies a 7 A recommended continuous path limit / 7 A continuous absolute-limit row and pulsed 10 A absolute maximum, but no programmable steady load-current limiter in this path. External load/charger current policy and source behavior remain necessary. [TI TPS25730 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps25730.pdf), sections 6.1.2, 6.3.1, 6.8, 6.10, 8.3.3.1.

It can replace **STUSB4500 + Q1/Q2 PMOS pair + U18 TPS26600 + U19 TPS7B84 LDO** with one TPS25730D controller/power-path device, plus its strap resistors and mandatory bypass/bulk capacitors. This removes the external 11 V OVP divider, ST VBUS sense/discharge/zener support and the dedicated PD 5 V LDO. The TPS device has its own VBUS-to-3.3 V dead-battery LDO. Its ADC straps configure sink min/max voltage among 5/9/12/15/20 V, source-power/current requirement and maximum current; 5 V default startup and autonomous sink operation are supported. It is a strong fit for broad *fixed SPR* PDO acceptance without MCU-dependent profile updates; no PPS or EPR should be assumed. The D variant integrates its path; S uses external FET gate drivers.

**Important limitations to settle before adopting it:**

- The D path's recommended VBUS operating maximum is 22 V and absolute maximum is 28 V. Its OVP threshold is configurable from 5 to 24 V, with a 2–4 µs fast path-off specification. For 20 V operation, configure OVP above the highest valid 20 V source tolerance/ripple but below device/system limits. This threshold only addresses sustained/fast OV after comparator response; it cannot guarantee survival of an input spike that already exceeds the 28 V absolute rating.
- Existing raw TVS2200 is 22 V standoff with a **28.4 V maximum clamp at 40 A**, above the TPS25730D 28 V abs max by 0.4 V before board inductance. TI's TPS25730 datasheet recommends a TVS with breakdown between 22 V recommended and 28 V absolute (giving TVS2200 as an example), but the TVS maximum-clamp and layout overshoot still need waveform/energy qualification. Do not claim surge compliance from the part pairing alone. The 20 V design has only 2 V headroom from valid maximum operating voltage to TPS25730D recommended 22 V input ceiling; include source tolerance and transition overshoot in review.
- TI TPS25730 guidance says the sink power path is **not automatically disabled** on capability mismatch; `CAP_MIS` reports the mismatch and `SINK_EN` reports path state. A TI support clarification reports CAP_MIS is tied to valid PD contracts and does not report USB Type-C current advertisement; for a non-PD connection, the part uses USB default-current behavior. Therefore don't use CAP_MIS alone as a safe low-current-control signal. STUSB4500 has optional `POWER_OK2/3` Type-C current class GPIO mode, but it is not enabled/routed in the active schematic and is not a verified I²C status register. If the product must exploit 1.5/3 A Type-C Rp or proprietary USB-A 5 V/2 A adapters, check actual controller GPIO/software support and legacy adapter detection in the integrated design. TPS25730's documented interfaces don't provide Rp class status.
- TPS25730D PPHV OVP/RCP is **not a programmable current limiter**. Configure the downstream charger/system current and audio power budget; test overload and source removal. Its `CAP_MIS` status does not enforce the requested power automatically. TI's CPPHV table recommends 47 µF nominal, leaves the minimum column blank and gives a 100 µF maximum; connector-side VBUS has a separate 1–10 µF recommendation and >100 µF after contract needs surge-current control. Rework inrush/bulk-cap planning rather than reusing arbitrary existing capacitor totals.

### Cost and decision

At this review's LCSC listing snapshot, TPS25730DREFR (C22438973, QFN-38 4×6 mm) showed **381 in stock at US$3.0105 for one** (10+ US$2.5779); availability is only a snapshot. Existing BOM snapshot unit costs were STUSB4500 US$2.6826 + two STL9P3LLH6 US$1.0781 each + TPS26600 US$1.6339 = **US$6.47** before U19 TPS7B84 (which becomes unnecessary for PD VDD) and support passives. Thus the TI integrated device could reduce captured major-part cost by roughly US$3.46 before support-part deltas and remove most of the PD-specific power path. The LTC4368-2 candidate totals about US$13.25 for controller + two FETs + shunt at its research snapshot, so TPS25730D has a substantial parts-cost advantage and comparable/lower room-temperature path resistance; LTC's advantage is 60 V controller/external-FET voltage headroom and customizable fast-breaker design, while its shunt and comparator are not a regulated current limit either.

**Selection judgment:** TPS25730D is the leading *hardware simplification / broad fixed-PDO* candidate if conservative non-PD fallback is acceptable, explicit-PD current is read before load enable, and attach/suspend policy is implemented separately. For USB-host attachment, the PCM2902C's current descriptor is 100 mA; this must not be confused with the controller's 500/900 mA USB-default sink behavior. The project must also decide if it needs Rp-based 1.5/3 A for non-PD Type-C sources; STUSB4500 can signal those classes through optional `POWER_OK2/3` pins, while TPS25730 has no documented Rp status. Keep controller selection conditional on the integrated current-status path and TVS/transient margins, with no assumption that a 5 V adapter label grants 2 A.

## TPS25730D I²C current-status check and cold-start gating

A review of TI's official TPS25730 Host Interface Technical Reference Manual resolves the GPIO-versus-I²C distinction. **TPS25730 does expose explicit-PD source and contract data over I²C, but it does not document a Type-C Rp-current status register.** The available documented register set is short: `RX_SOURCE_CAPS` at `0x30` is updated after the `GSrC` (Get Source Capabilities) task; `ACTIVE_CONTRACT_PDO` at `0x34` and `ACTIVE_CONTRACT_RDO` at `0x35` report the source PDO selected and accepted RDO for the current explicit PD contract. The RDO includes object position, capability-mismatch, `NoUSBSuspend`, operating current and maximum operating current fields. These let STM32 firmware read the accepted PD current/power contract before allowing a charger/load policy to increase current. `RX_SINK_CAPS` `0x31` is the opposite-direction sink-capabilities receive buffer; `TX_SINK_CAPS` `0x33` is the sink-capability policy advertised by this port. **No `PORT_STATUS`, `TYPEC_STATUS`, Rp-current or CC-current register at `0x3F/0x40` appears in this TPS25730 TRM**; those addresses/register names belong to other controller families and should not be assumed here. [TI TPS25730 TRM, SLVUCJ7](https://www.ti.com/lit/ug/slvucj7/slvucj7.pdf), §§2.1–2.4, 3.2.1.

TI support confirms `CAP_MIS` and `SINK_EN` are the only status outputs beyond the limited TRM data; CAP_MIS does not report an implicit Type-C (non-PD) contract and does not disable PPHV when mismatch occurs. TI's 500 mA USB2 / 900 mA USB3 statement is a controller's conservative non-PD sink ceiling, **not** permission for a USB host before enumeration. The current PCM2902C descriptor is 100 mA; apply 100 mA at attach/pre-configuration and define suspend current separately. For the planned BQ25792, firmware must set source-aware charger limits after reading an explicit PD contract; its charger interface does not replace USB CC/Rp sensing. Confirm BQ25792 input-current setup and default-off sequencing in the charger integration review. The active BQ25895 still uses separate /CE and ILIM behavior until replacement. [TI support: TPS25730 power detection](https://e2e.ti.com/support/power-management-group/power-management/f/power-management-forum/1318693/tps25730-about-power-detection), [TI support: status reporting limits](https://e2e.ti.com/support/power-management-group/power-management/f/power-management-forum/1395446/tps25730-the-register-map-to-show-device-status).

An MCU-gated cold start can still make the **PD explicit-contract** path orderly: the TPS25730 dead-battery configuration presents Rd and starts from VBUS; its current `AlwaysEnableSink` setting closes the integrated sink path before runtime configuration has loaded. Keep the planned BQ25792 charge/load path hardware-disabled by its reviewed default, and keep audio/other high-load rails off. Once the STM32 starts, it can inspect the active contract via I²C, program BQ25792 input-current/charge policy and enable charging/system load only after a valid contract and limits are confirmed. VBUS itself remains present during boot, and `SINK_EN` is path state—not a PD-ready indication. Confirm exact BQ25792 default-off/current programming from the charger review, and ensure no bypass load exceeds the attach/suspend/contract budget before firmware acts. The active BQ25895 schematic instead has /CE and analog ILIM; it has no ILIM_HIZ pin.

For dead-battery power, TPS25730's internal VBUS LDO can power its own core and offers LDO_3V3. The datasheet's VBUS-LDO output recommendation is **up to 5 mA external load**, whereas with an external VIN_3V3 the LDO_3V3 output supports up to 18 mA in the cited condition. Measure/bound STM32G031 boot plus I²C pulls and any other circuitry against the 5 mA dead-battery budget. If the MCU cannot guarantee that margin, retain a separate raw-VBUS regulator for the MCU rather than assuming the TPS LDO has ordinary system-rail capacity. [TI TPS25730 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps25730.pdf), §§6.3, 6.7, 8.3.2.

**Architecture update:** TPS25730 can provide accepted PDO/RDO current for explicit PD before the MCU enables charger/system loads. It cannot report non-PD Rp current through its documented I²C/GPIO interfaces, so keep attach/suspend policy conservative; the current PCM2902C descriptor is 100 mA for a USB host. For fixed 5–20 V SPR, TPS25730D is the preferred controller candidate on autonomy, integrated path loss and BOM if this policy is acceptable. STUSB4500 can signal Rp classes through optional `POWER_OK2/3` GPIO mode, but that status path is not yet confirmed as wired/configured for MCU in this design; use it only after integration review verifies the interface. BQ25792 is the planned direct charger input; no pre-buck is planned. The active BQ25895 sheet remains a 5/9 V implementation until replacement integration.

D versus S remains a surge-path choice. TPS25730D is the simplest, lower-resistance integrated switch. TPS25730S uses external back-to-back N-FETs and therefore lets the switch devices have higher VDS/SOA ratings, but the controller's own VBUS input remains 28 V absolute / 22 V recommended, so S does not cure raw controller-pin TVS margin. **TI explicitly recommends the TVS2200 as an example** in the TPS25730 datasheet's VBUS-pin note (a TVS breakdown voltage between recommended and absolute limits). TVS2200 is consequently a manufacturer-suggested pairing, not an arbitrary mismatch: it specifies 22 V standoff, 24.6–27.6 V breakdown, and clamp 27.6–28 V at 40 A/27°C or up to 28.35 V at 35 A/125°C while biased at standoff. The latter worst-case specified clamp slightly exceeds TPS25730's 28 V absolute maximum. The TPS datasheet does not state a transient allowance above that absolute rating. Thus this pairing is a credible TI design starting point, but not a guaranteed pin-voltage margin from worst-case tabular limits; keep TVS directly at the connector with a very short, wide return/ground loop and route the protected VBUS node compactly. Qualification should measure VBUS at the TPS pin during the product's specified surge/hot-plug tests and check it remains within ratings; TI's TVS2200 IEC surge qualification alone does not qualify the TPS pin or this board. Do not put a second series eFuse in front of TPS25730 solely to resolve the 0.35 V table-limit discrepancy without first testing the TI-recommended direct clamp. If measured overshoot fails, an upstream 60 V controller plus external 60 V NFETs (e.g. the reviewed LTC4368-2 architecture) can isolate the TPS VBUS node, but its ~$13.25 protection BOM plus TPS25730D (~$3.01) is a large cost increase and needs weak-5-V dropout validation. A different external-FET TPS25730S does not solve raw-controller-pin exposure. No alternative TVS with guaranteed 22 V standoff and <=27.5 V maximum clamp was qualified in this review. [TI TPS25730 datasheet, §6.1.1/footnote and §9.2](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps25730.pdf), [TI TVS2200 datasheet, §§7.1, 7.6, 8.4](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tvs2200.pdf).

### Surge decision and 5 V legacy-source policy

Use TPS25730D + the TI-recommended TVS2200 as the preferred low-loss, low-part-count prototype architecture for 5–20 V SPR; make worst-case VBUS-at-controller surge measurement a release gate. The TPS25730 has no separate specified transient-above-absolute allowance. Its 22 V recommended maximum leaves only 2 V above nominal negotiated 20 V, so also account for source regulation, cable/hot-plug ringing and any configured OVP threshold. Keep the TVS close to the Type-C connector, minimize the high-current TVS-to-ground return inductance, and follow TI's connector-side 1–10 µF and protected PPHV-side 47 µF nominal recommendation (100 µF max; no minimum specified); do not enlarge pre-contract capacitance casually. This direct arrangement is preferable to stacking an eFuse that adds loss/cost. If the product surge target or measured waveform requires a hard guaranteed sub-28 V raw-node clamp, the available review has not established a simple drop-in solution; use a higher-voltage-rated isolating front end, or select a controller with higher raw-VBUS rating, then re-evaluate cost and 5 V drop.

For BQ25792 legacy USB-A / BC1.2 operation, distinguish the charger's detector presets from what a source is allowed to supply. TI's D+/D− table maps a standard BC1.2 DCP to a 3.25 A detector setting and HVDCP to 1.5 A; the 3.25 A entry is not blanket permission to draw 3.25 A from any DCP adapter. Apply a conservative **1.5 A ceiling** to an ordinary BC1.2 DCP unless a separately detected/validated source advertises a higher current. A generic “5 V / 2 A” label does not make all USB-A ports or cables 2 A capable. Use a higher BQ25792 IINDPM only when detection/profile or product policy supports it; budget input current for the whole system, not just battery charge. USB-host SDP must follow enumeration: this product's PCM2902C descriptor is 100 mA, so stay at 100 mA before configuration and define suspend separately. [TI BQ25792 datasheet, §9.2/table 9-4](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/bq25792.pdf).
