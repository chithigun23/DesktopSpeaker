# Limited 5 V bootstrap path review

Updated: 2026-10-05. Research only; no schematic or firmware changes. Reviewed the proposed raw-VBUS → TPS7B8450-Q1 5 V LDO → TPS22946 (CL low, nominal 70 mA mode) → BQ25895 path, in parallel with a default-off LTC4368 main path.

## Parts and constraints

- **TPS7B8450QWDRBRQ1**, LCSC **C3751394**, is the fixed 5 V, 150 mA, 40 V input TPS7B84-Q1 variant in 3 × 3 mm VSON-8. The LCSC listing was found; live inventory was not established. TI Rev. B datasheet (`../datasheets/TPS7B84-Q1.pdf`) gives 3–40 V operating input, 5 V fixed output, ±0.75% output accuracy, max 225 mV dropout at 150 mA for the DRB package, and 2.2 µF minimum output capacitance. Its pass element does **not** block reverse current; TI warns reverse current is not limited and external protection is needed when output can be biased above input. At 4.45 V input and a 70 mA load, the regulator will be in dropout; typical resistance scaling suggests roughly 0.1 V drop, but a guaranteed worst-case output voltage at that low input/load corner was not found. Budget diode and switch drops separately before claiming BQ VBUS headroom.
- At 9 V input, LDO dissipation is `(9 − 5) × IOUT`: 0.28 W at 70 mA and 0.46 W at 115 mA, plus quiescent loss. The DRB datasheet θJA is 50.8 °C/W on the specified board; at 70 mA this corresponds to roughly 14 °C junction rise over ambient in that board condition. Thermal behavior must be recomputed for actual copper, ambient, and continuous boot-rail loads. Do not allow an unintended 20 V PD contract to this branch; verify STUSB4500 only requests the selected 5/9 V PDOs and retain the raw-bus OVP.
- **TPS22946YZPR**, LCSC **C130053**, is an available 6-ball DSBGA (0.9 × 1.4 mm) candidate; the LCSC listing showed 235 units at the observed snapshot, not a future stock guarantee. TI Rev. B datasheet is saved as `../datasheets/TI_TPS22946.pdf`. It accepts 1.62–5.5 V and has 300 mΩ typical RON at 5.5 V. However “70 mA” is the selectable **minimum** current limit: the electrical table specifies 60–115 mA at VIN=3.3 V/VOUT=3.0 V and 70–120 mA at VIN=1.8 V/VOUT=1.5 V over full range. This cannot guarantee total connector current ≤100 mA even before STUSB4500 or other raw-input loads are counted.
- More critically, TPS22946 permits a **435 mA typical / 685 mA maximum** inrush-limit window for 8 ms after enabling, then current limiting; it is explicitly designed to allow startup inrush above its steady setting. Therefore it cannot be described as a strict ≤100 mA cold-start path unless the current requirement expressly excludes this interval and a separate upstream source limiter constrains it. An LDO current rating of 150 mA does not turn the 435 mA transient allowance into a specified ≤100 mA USB input cap. Persistent overcurrent eventually turns the switch off and auto-retries; after 5 seconds the device latches disabled until ON is cycled.
- TPS22946 does **not** provide reverse-current blocking. TI states its off switch body diode does not prevent reverse current. A 9 V main path connected to the common BQ VBUS can backfeed the nominal 5 V bootstrap output, elevating TPS22946 OUT and the LDO output above their expected level. Add a suitably rated reverse-blocking element between the bootstrap switch and common BQ input, or select a load switch/eFuse with guaranteed reverse blocking. A series diode would need reverse-voltage margin above the maximum protected bus/transient and its forward drop must be included in the weak-5 V startup calculation; at 4.45 V raw input the headroom is already tight.

## Current budget and BQ behavior

The TPS22946 branch limit applies only to current passing through that switch. STUSB4500 and any raw-VBUS-powered indicators or other blocks bypass it and add to the connector total. If the MCU/control supply is derived from BQ `SYS`/`3VAO`, it also loads BQ input in addition to the 30 mA source qualification pulse. Measure or bound each term in the fallback budget; the BQ IINLIM number is a charger setting, not a source grant.

The 30 mA TPS26620 setting discussed in the preceding report is a different limiter and should not be conflated with this branch. TPS22946’s 30 mA mode has a 60 mA maximum in the documented 3.3 V/3.0 V full-range condition; it could be investigated if its inrush window is acceptable and all other source loads leave margin, but it still is not a strict cold-attach 100 mA ceiling due to the 8 ms boost. Do not assume the BQ's startup clause means it forcibly demands 200 mA: it limits converter startup current to the lower of 200 mA and IINLIM while SYS is below 2.2 V. Whether a specific cap/load combination starts correctly through a smaller source limiter needs bench validation. BQ25895’s typical 30 mA poor-source pulse should be supplied without collapsing VBUS before DPDM classification.

## Architecture conclusion

The proposed LDO makes the low-voltage switch safe from 9 V/20 V at its input and offers a plausible control/bootstrap rail, but the TPS22946 70 mA mode is not a bounded 70 mA ceiling and its 8 ms inrush behavior breaks a strict low-current startup claim. Its lack of reverse blocking also makes a 5 V bootstrap branch unsafe to parallel directly with a 9 V main path. Keep the LTC4368 path default-off in hardware through reset/brownout; only enable after volatile classification and policy commit. Do not represent this candidate as a proven conservative USB fallback until current overshoot, aggregate raw draw, reverse isolation, 4.45 V weak-source startup, and BQ source qualification are resolved and verified.
