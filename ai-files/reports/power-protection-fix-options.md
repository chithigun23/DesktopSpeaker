# USB PD protection and 5 V loss options — 2026-10-05

Architecture research only. No schematic, library, BOM or plan edits. This compares replacements for the present Q1/Q2 STL9P3LLH6 reverse-blocking pair followed by U18 TPS26600 and D8 on `VBUS_PD`; BQ25895 remains the 1S charger. The goal is normal USB/USB-PD adapter use, not an industrial surge rating. Final choice still needs schematic integration, PCB layout and waveform validation.

**Revision note:** the follow-up section below supersedes the initial TPS25947/TPS25948 replacement recommendation for any design retaining the raw 22 V-standoff / 28.4 V-clamp TVS2200. Those eFuses have 28 V absolute maximum and therefore do not provide a defensible input-voltage margin against that clamp. Use the LTC4368 or TPS2663 evaluation below for the higher-voltage input path, subject to their stated architectural and 5 V limitations.

## Recommendation

**Practical first choice: retain Q1/Q2 and replace U18 with TI TPS259470ARPWR (LCSC C3662799), configured as an adjustable OVLO cutoff, not its OVC clamp mode.** It operates from 2.7–23 V, has 28 V absolute maximum, integrated reverse-blocking / reverse-polarity protection, 45 mΩ maximum on-resistance over its stated voltage and temperature range, adjustable output slew rate and a typical 1.2 µs OVLO response. This directly replaces the 250 mΩ-max TPS26600 stage while leaving STUSB4500's existing controllable sink switch unchanged. LCSC listed the QFN part in stock on review date; stock and price are volatile. [TI TPS25947 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps25947.pdf), [LCSC listing](https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/C3662799.html).

Set the cutoff nominally around **10.4–10.5 V**, with resistor/reference corners proven above the maximum valid 9 V contract (allow at least 9.45 V for a 9 V ±5% source) and below BQ25895's 14 V recommended input maximum. Example divider scale only: a 1.2 V OVLO threshold and 100 kΩ / 13 kΩ top/bottom is about 10.43 V nominal; calculate actual min/max using TI's latest threshold limits, resistor tolerance/TCR, and the chosen variant before capture. Set an independent input UVLO around **4.4 V at the eFuse input** as a starting point, then verify VBUS drop at the BQ pins under load. Choose eFuse current threshold around 2.2 A nominal only as a fault ceiling above the charger's existing ≤1.77 A hardware ILIM ceiling; it does not grant source current. Keep source-aware BQ limits and load sequencing as separate required controls.

**Why this is a useful compromise:** no package technology change beyond a 2×2 mm HotRod QFN; it adds low-loss reverse blocking and reverse-polarity handling; and a resistor divider can block sustained 15/20 V faults. TPS25947 OVLO response is specified only as a **typical** 1.2 µs, not a worst-case maximum. It cannot alone guarantee a live 9→20 V step never exceeds BQ25895's 16 V switching-pin absolute maximum. Keep D8 and local capacitance as a secondary clamp/energy store, then scope U4 VBUS and SW during worst-case source transitions.

A further loss reduction is possible if the chosen eFuse can also replace Q1/Q2: use its integrated reverse-blocking FET as the sink switch and drive EN/UVLO from a properly level-adapted STUSB4500 `VBUS_EN_SNK` signal. Do not connect that signal directly without verifying voltage, polarity, startup ordering and dead-battery behavior. Removing the 45 mΩ PMOS allowance would improve the same 4.75 V / 0.20 Ω / 1.5 A case by another 68 mV: actual calculations are about 4.383 V for TPS25947-only and 4.420 V for TPS25948-only. This is a second-phase architecture option, because it changes the USB sink-switch sequencing rather than a simple U18 replacement.

Avoid TPS25947's TPS259472 OVC clamp variant for the main recommendation: its 13.8 V nominal clamp has a specified 13.0–14.6 V range at only 10 mA. A sustained 20 V input would make the eFuse pass FET dissipate substantial linear power, and 14.6 V does not leave a guaranteed margin to the BQ25895 14 V recommended maximum. Use the adjustable **cutoff** variant instead.

## Series-drop comparison

Use the existing design-review allowances for the paired PMOS (45 mΩ total; still not a hot/low-gate-drive guarantee) and 0.20 Ω cable/contact loop. Compare worst-case device resistances where the datasheet provides them. The “old” path is 250 mΩ TPS26600 max + 45 mΩ PMOS allowance = 295 mΩ. The QFN TPS25947 path is 45 mΩ max + 45 mΩ PMOS allowance = 90 mΩ. The WLCSP TPS25948 path is 20 mΩ max + 45 mΩ PMOS allowance = 65 mΩ.

| Source/cable scenario | Current | Present path at BQ VBUS | TPS25947 + PMOS | TPS25948 + PMOS |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4.75 V source, 0.20 Ω loop | 1.50 A | 4.008 V | 4.315 V | 4.353 V |
| 4.75 V source, 0.20 Ω loop | 1.77 A | 3.874 V | 4.237 V | 4.281 V |
| Board-only drop (no cable) | 1.50 A | 0.443 V | 0.135 V | 0.098 V |
| Board-only loss | 1.50 A | 0.664 W | 0.203 W | 0.147 W |
| Board-only drop (no cable) | 1.77 A | 0.522 V | 0.159 V | 0.115 V |
| Board-only loss | 1.77 A | 0.924 W | 0.282 W | 0.203 W |

These use `V_BQ = V_source − I × (R_loop + R_board)`. The 4.75 V / 0.20 Ω case is illustrative, not a compliance envelope; ignore connector/PCB resistance, temperature and cable variation beyond that explicit loop. It shows the current eFuse can take the BQ input below its 3.9 V operating minimum at 1.77 A; either replacement restores useful margin in the same scenario, but no 5 V / 1.77 A loudness or charging claim follows. Actual current policy, cable and thermal behavior still govern.

For the external-controller option, an illustrative 20 mΩ current-sense resistor (about 2.5 A nominal at the LTC4368's 50 mV forward trip) plus a selected 4 mΩ total back-to-back N-FET path gives about 24 mΩ board path. At 1.5 A this is about 36 mV / 54 mW, or about 4.414 V at the charger for the same 4.75 V/0.20 Ω source. This estimate excludes layout, shunt tolerance, comparator threshold tolerance, MOSFET hot RDS(on), and hot-swap/inrush SOA. It is a design target, not a guaranteed result. The controller's 32 ms turn-on delay and sense trip tolerance need system validation.

The eFuse/controller input-side quiescent current is drawn from external VBUS, not BAT_PACK, in this topology. With the battery-only input disconnected, it should not add directly to the battery standby budget; verify reverse leakage through the complete path. TPS25947/48 enabled quiescent is roughly 0.43 mA typical and LTC4368 about 80 µA typical while the adapter path is powered. The 5 µA LTC shutdown rating is not the powered-on current.

## Clamp and live-transition coordination

- **Raw USB VBUS TVS:** retain a ≥22 V working-standoff class part such as the selected TI TVS2200 for the raw connector node. Note the new TPS25947 also has a 28 V absolute maximum, so D6’s 28.4 V maximum clamp at rated surge does not guarantee its survival at the full TVS-rated surge either; TPS25948’s IEC “28 V on IN” test is a stronger component-level data point, but does not raise the STUSB4500 pin rating or establish immunity to every setup. A lower 18–20 V stand-off risks loading a legitimate 20 V PD contract, especially with source tolerance; do not “fix” the 28 V clamp number by lowering TVS stand-off blindly. TVS2200's 28.4 V maximum clamp is specified at its rated surge current and exceeds STUSB4500's 28 V absolute maximum, so it does **not** establish full-rated surge protection for STUSB4500. Because the project does not claim an industrial surge level, define the intended consumer adapter/cable envelope and verify realistic hot-plug/contract transitions at the STUSB4500 pins. If that measured edge can exceed 28 V, use a controller/pin arrangement with more voltage margin or added current-limited sensing protection; do not claim the TVS alone guarantees it.
- **Protected charger input:** keep a 10 V working-standoff TVS class on `VBUS_PD` (D8 SMA6J10A) so normal 9 V operation is not clamped. Its 15.7 V max clamp at 38.2 A has only 0.3 V nominal room to BQ25895 SW's 16 V absolute maximum, and is not a proof of safe live switching. Lowering to a 9 V standoff TVS can conflict with high-tolerance 9 V PD; raising the standoff weakens the clamp. Prefer the fast adjustable eFuse cutoff near 10.4–10.5 V plus close placement of the eFuse, D8, and charger bypass capacitors. Scope U4 VBUS and both SW pins during 5→9, 9→20 and removal/reconnect transitions with worst-case realistic cable/source impedance.
- **OVLO edge:** both proposed eFuses publish typical (not guaranteed maximum) OVLO response times. The downstream peak depends on source rise time, input/output capacitance, TVS dynamic impedance, ground inductance and eFuse shutdown dynamics. BQ25895's 14 V recommendation applies in operation; its 22 V VBUS absolute rating is for the converter not switching, so it cannot be used as proof that a transition during switching is safe.
- **Protection topology:** retain the raw-side TVS and the protected-side TVS as distinct functions. The former passes 20 V without conduction; the eFuse cuts off sustained overvoltage; the latter absorbs residual output energy. No one device's “clamp” or absolute rating substitutes for the full path transient test.

## Follow-up: obtain real input-voltage margin — 2026-10-05

This follow-up is prompted by the requirement that an eFuse replacement retain useful margin above the 22 V-standoff raw TVS, whose rated maximum clamp is 28.4 V. No project files other than this report were changed.

### Findings and revised ranking

1. **Best protection and low-drop candidate: ADI LTC4368-2 plus an external back-to-back N-FET pair and sense resistor, re-architecting Q1/Q2/U18 as one input path.** It operates from 2.5–60 V and withstands −40 V to +100 V at VIN, so 28.4 V has ample controller rating margin. OV/UV thresholds are adjustable (±1.5%); the 50 mV forward circuit-breaker threshold and LTC4368-2's −3 mV reverse threshold support bidirectional current fault protection. Typical active current is 80 µA and shutdown current 5 µA. A first-pass 20 mΩ shunt gives a 2.5 A typical circuit-breaker trip; a MOSFET pair with 4 mΩ combined hot resistance yields approximately 24 mΩ path, or 36 mV / 54 mW at 1.5 A. With a 4.75 V connector source and explicit 0.20 Ω cable/contact loop, that path estimates 4.414 V at the charger at 1.5 A (4.354 V at 1.77 A). This avoids the TPS2663's 4.5 V minimum-input corner in that cable case. It is a circuit breaker, not an active limiter: threshold tolerance, shunt, MOSFET SOA, inrush slew and hot plug must be designed. It introduces a 32 ms turn-on/recovery delay and requires replacing the PMOS switch path with a topology controlled by the STUSB4500 sink-enable signal (level, polarity and startup ordering still need a schematic-level review). **It is the strongest architecture candidate, not a drop-in recommendation.** [ADI LTC4368 datasheet](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/ltc4368.pdf).

2. **Simpler integrated-eFuse candidate if the cable drop is bounded: TI TPS26630RGER, with external adjustable OVP, retaining Q1/Q2.** TPS2663 family operates 4.5–60 V, has 67 V absolute maximum, 30.44 mΩ typical / 53 mΩ maximum IN-to-OUT resistance across −40 to +125 °C, and 0.6–6 A adjustable current limit. Its OVP comparator responds in 8.5–14 µs; overvoltage is cutoff for TPS26630 (avoid clamp-only logic). At the 28.4 V TVS clamp, this gives 38.6 V margin to abs max. Replacing the 250 mΩ-max TPS26600 drops the assumed board resistance from 295 mΩ to at most 98 mΩ including the existing 45 mΩ PMOS allowance; at 1.5 A the board-only loss estimate falls from 0.664 W to 0.221 W. But TPS2663's operating minimum is 4.5 V: with a 4.75 V connector, 0.20 Ω external loop and 45 mΩ PMOS pair, the eFuse input would be only 4.38 V at 1.5 A, below its operating range. It therefore cannot meet that explicit weak-source case without also removing the PMOS drop, shortening the cable, or relaxing the source corner. It draws 1.38 mA typical / 1.7 mA maximum while enabled; shutdown is 21 µA typical / 60 µA maximum. A battery-only reverse-leakage budget still needs full-path verification. TPS26630 has a built-in 34.33 V nominal OVP when its OVP select input chooses the factory threshold; the design would need the specified external OVP divider to set a lower cutoff. [TI TPS2663 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps2663.pdf). LCSC listing for TPS26633RGER showed 2 units on 2026-10-05, but that is the power-limiting TPS26633 variant and is not the preferred exact choice; check stock for TPS26630RGER at order time.

3. **Reject TPS25982/83 for this raw-clamp coordination.** TPS25982 has 2.7–24 V recommended input; TPS25983 has 2.7–26 V. Both have 30 V absolute maximum and 2.7 mΩ typical ON resistance; their current-limit adjustment starts at 2 A. The 28.4 V rated TVS clamp is only 1.6 V below their absolute maximum and is above the recommended operating limit for both, so this is not defensible live-edge or TVS-rated surge headroom. They also do not provide a guaranteed maximum OVP shutdown time for this use in the cited product summaries. Their attractive low resistance does not solve the actual protection gap. [TI TPS25982](https://www.ti.com/product/TPS25982), [TI TPS25983](https://www.ti.com/product/TPS25983), [TVS2200 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tvs2200.pdf).

### STUSB4500 supply-pin fix

**Do not use the proposed VSYS-only / VDD-NC wiring as the first choice. Use a raw-VBUS-fed 5.0 V high-voltage LDO into VDD and ground VSYS, preserving ST's VDD-only topology.** The STUSB4500 datasheet says it can be supplied by either VDD or VSYS but explicitly says VDD-only is mandatory for VBUS input-voltage monitoring; its dead-battery startup description expects the part to be powered from the connector-side VBUS path after unpowered CC1DB/CC2DB Rd detection. VDD operating range is 4.1–22 V, VSYS is 3.0–5.5 V, and VDD absolute max is 28 V. Thus a raw-VBUS-fed **TPS7A1650** (5.0 V fixed, 3–60 V input, 100 mA output, 5 µA IQ, 1 µA shutdown) can keep the ST pin at 5 V even when the raw TVS clamps at 28.4 V. At a USB 5 V source, the LDO is in dropout, but a nominal 4.75 V connector input minus the TI LDO's 60 mV typical dropout at 20 mA is still about 4.69 V, above ST's 4.1 V VDD minimum; characterize actual minimum source/cable voltage and startup. Keep the existing `VBUS_VS_DISCH` sensing network on the protected/controlled rail (the project review describes it after the eFuse; ensure routing stays there), so that pin sees valid negotiated voltage rather than raw clamp. The LDO only consumes a few hundred µA when USB is present and has no direct battery-only input when disconnected. The existing unpowered Rd strap is still needed; confirm CCDB wiring and NVM boot behavior during integration. This is a datasheet-grounded candidate; verify ST's exact multi-supply wiring and startup after changing U11 VDD in the schematic. [ST STUSB4500 datasheet](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/stusb4500.pdf), [TI TPS7A16 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps7a16.pdf), [LCSC TPS7A1650DRBR C544778](https://www.lcsc.com/de/product-detail/C544778.html) (listed 465 pcs 2026-10-05).

### Coordination arithmetic and limitations

- TVS2200 remains 22 V working standoff / 28.4 V maximum clamp at its specified rated pulse. Do not lower its standoff below the maximum legitimate 20 V contract. A 60 V-rated LDO/controller improves silicon voltage headroom but does not itself prove TVS response, layout overshoot, or protection during all adapter hot-plug profiles.
- For the LTC4368 path, use input MOSFET VDS rating with margin above the TVS maximum plus measured inductive overshoot; use a protected-node TVS only after reconciling the selected PD maximum and BQ25895's 14 V recommended input limit. A 20 V contract cannot be passed directly into BQ25895 operation. The source PDO policy must be settled before programming OVLO.
- For TPS26630, an OVP cutoff set above the maximum allowed input contract and below the charger-safe region must account for resistor/reference tolerance and response delay. If the project must accept 15/20 V PD, this eFuse cannot simultaneously protect a directly connected BQ25895 operating input; that is an architecture mismatch, independent of the eFuse choice.
- Recomputed board-only at 1.5 A: old 295 mΩ path = 0.443 V / 0.664 W; TPS2663 plus 45 mΩ PMOS = 98 mΩ = 0.147 V / 0.221 W; LTC4368 example 24 mΩ = 0.036 V / 0.054 W. The LTC figure is a component-selection target, not a guaranteed finished-path result.
