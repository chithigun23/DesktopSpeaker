# Limited bootstrap integration review

Reviewed 2026-10-05 against the existing MAX4995, TPS7B84-Q1, BQ25895 and LTC4368 reports and local primary datasheets. Research only; no schematic/library/shared-document edits.

## Assessment

The proposed `raw USB → TPS7B8450 5 V LDO → MAX4995B (390 kΩ RSET) → series blocking diode → BQ25895 VBUS`, alongside a default-off LTC4368-2 main path, is **not a guaranteed ≤100 mA cold-attach design as drawn**. The MAX4995B does provide a defensible bound on current through the BQ branch after its input is powered: datasheet limit equation plus 1% RSET gives about 66.6–82.2 mA over specified conditions. It does not bound the complete connector draw or guarantee that the battery-absent charger sequence succeeds.

- **Battery absent:** BQ25895 applies a poor-source test pulse of 30 mA *typical*; the datasheet gives no min/max for that pulse or guaranteed source-qualification threshold. With SYS below 2.2 V, converter startup input current is limited to `min(200 mA, IINLIM)`, which is a ceiling, not a demand guarantee. The MAX branch's 82.2 mA maximum may accommodate the typical pulse, but datasheet limits do not prove cold-start margin for BQ VBUS/SYS capacitance, charger startup, and the absent-battery case. This needs bench qualification across source and temperature corners.
- **100 mA legacy USB budget / attach inrush:** 100 mA is not an instantaneous attach ceiling. USB-IF's current inrush test captures at least 100 ms and evaluates charge above 100 mA separately for each excursion/region; pass/fail uses the region with the largest charge. Thus the presence of the LDO output capacitor upstream of MAX4995 does **not by itself establish noncompliance**. It does establish an inrush source that must be measured and analyzed with USBET. Existing C2=0.1 µF, C4=1 µF, C6=1 µF plus C5=4.7 µF reused as the LDO output capacitor total **6.8 µF nominal**; do not add a second 4.7 µF capacitor to the same rail. At initial 5 V attach, +20% tolerance and `Q=C·V` give a conservative **41 µC whole-charge bound** for those four capacitors. C1 and C3 are separate STUSB internal-rail bypasses charged through VDD, not unconditional 5 V VBUS capacitance; including both 1 µF parts as an additional conservative supply-startup charge budget would raise the simple bound to 53 µC. These bounds are not USBET charge-above-100-mA results. Add measured startup current/charge from LDO, STUSB4500 and other raw loads. TPS7B84's 180–260 mA current limit means this transient is not bounded to 100 mA by that regulator, but USB-IF permits excursions if largest-region charge passes. LTC4368 is a circuit breaker, not an attach limiter.
- **Output isolation diode:** it is required. MAX4995 IN operates only to 5.5 V and OUT absolute maximum is 6 V; its reverse-current protection does not make OUT safe when the parallel main path raises BQ VBUS to 9 V. Orient the diode anode at MAX4995 OUT and cathode at the shared BQ VBUS. Its **reverse-voltage rating must exceed the maximum actual protected bus plus credible transients**, using the worst-case LTC4368 OV divider threshold/tolerance and downstream fault envelope. The current candidate's nominal 10.5 V OV is not itself a maximum rating. A 30 V diode may be adequate only after that envelope is calculated. Include diode forward drop at ≤~82 mA and low temperature in weak-5-V startup; existing 4.75 V estimates leave modest margin and do not establish a guaranteed BQ input voltage.
- **LDO inrush and headroom:** TPS7B84 current limit is 180–260 mA, far above the desired total fallback budget. Its 2.2 µF output cap is on the raw side of the MAX switch; downstream capacitance is limited by MAX4995 but that upstream capacitance is not. The attach waveform must be analyzed under the USB-IF charge-based inrush method; this transient alone does not prove failure. At low 5 V input, dropout is also a concern: the cited 225 mV maximum is specified at 150 mA, not a dedicated weak-source/bootstrap corner; combine guaranteed LDO output, cable sag, switch drop, diode drop, and BQ threshold before asserting startup margin.

The branch is electrically plausible as a **source-qualified, current-limited BQ feed** once the main path is controlled. It is not yet a proven cold-start fallback: branch current is bounded, but startup success is unknown and attach behavior is unmeasured. Its LDO, high-cost MAX switch, diode and sequencing may be more parts than needed, but the attach capacitor alone is not grounds to reject it under USB-IF's charge-based test.

## Simpler alternative and next step

An independently powered low-Iq MCU rail from raw 5 V could let firmware hold the BQ main path off and establish policy before enabling the LTC4368. It is a possible sequencing simplification, not a complete bootstrap solution: an absent battery may leave the MCU unable to configure an unpowered BQ over I²C; BQ DPDM source detection still requires BQ VBUS and its startup behavior remains the unresolved issue. Any raw-side MCU/LDO/STUSB loads also contribute to attach charge and USB suspend current. USB-IF states the USB 2.0 suspend maximum current is 2.5 mA; budget the actual raw-side suspend load against it. No independent-rail alternative is shown to solve BQ cold-start/source classification without an input current-limited BQ feed or separate detection mechanism.

### BQ24392 dedicated legacy BC1.2 detector candidate

TI's BQ24392 combines BC1.2 detection with an inline D+/D− USB2.0 switch, so it can classify legacy SDP/CDP/DCP before the BQ25895 is powered. Candidate orderable **BQ24392RSER**, LCSC **C128408** (LCSC snapshot reported 1,699 in stock at about US$0.65 each; recheck before purchase). TI Rev. G datasheet: `https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/bq24392.pdf`. Pin interface: DP_CON/DM_CON (pins 7/8) from connector; DP_HOST/DM_HOST (3/2) to PCM2902C; VBUS (9) supply; CHG_DET push-pull (10), CHG_AL_N open-drain (4), SW_OPEN open-drain (1), GOOD_BAT input (5), GND (6). CHG_AL_N/SW_OPEN require pullups. `CHG_DET=0` means SDP/not detected; `CHG_DET=1` means a charger was detected; `SW_OPEN=0` means data switch closed. With GOOD_BAT high, SDP and CDP close the switch while DCP leaves it open. CHG_DET alone does not distinguish CDP from DCP; the switch-state output does. The detector's source-type pins do not distinguish BC1.2 DCP from supported Apple/TomTom signatures, so a conservative policy should not grant 1.5 A solely from `CHG_DET=1, SW_OPEN=high-Z`; cap that ambiguous case or add independent classification. SDP remains capped at 100 mA total; BQ24392 is a detector/data switch, not a power limiter.

This can remove the MAX4995 BQ-bootstrap branch for **legacy SDP operation**: raw USB → protected 5 V auxiliary rail → PCM2902C and low-Iq 3 V MCU regulator; keep BQ input feed disabled on SDP and until policy allows it. After classification, the MCU can drive GOOD_BAT high when the system is ready for data; the pin has an internal pull-down and low keeps the data switch open. Then SDP audio can run from the auxiliary rail within the 100 mA total SDP budget, with no BQ boot needed. TI's PCM2902C power-supply table gives 67mA maximum for ADC/DAC operation under its stated test conditions. Budget the complete board separately; this codec figure is not a whole-product current bound. Add MCU, regulators, detector and STUSB load to the host budget. Keep main BQ/LTC path off for SDP so BQ reset 500 mA behavior cannot overload the host.

**Important voltage limitation:** BQ24392 has no separate low-voltage VDD pin; VBUS is its supply. Recommended VBUS is 4.75–5.25 V (28 V is only an absolute maximum). It cannot be connected directly to a 9 V PD rail, so powering it from the regulated 5 V auxiliary LDO is appropriate after a PD contract. But a TPS7B8450 on a weak source does not guarantee the detector's 4.75 V minimum: at 4.75 V LDO input, using its 225 mV max-dropout row gives only 4.525 V; at 4.35 V cable-end input that same conservative bound is 4.125 V. These are below BQ24392 recommended operating voltage, so legacy detection may fail exactly at the weak-cable corner unless a low-dropout/boosted auxiliary supply is chosen and qualified. PCM2902C's stated VBUS operating minimum is 4.35 V; the same 4.125 V conservative estimate is below that too. An LDO alone therefore has not been shown to keep either device in spec at the 4.35 V corner. At 9 V PD, 150 mA auxiliary-rail load dissipates about 0.6 W in a linear regulator, so use-case duty/thermal budget also matters.

Detection takes at least 130 ms and may take up to 600 ms (DCD slow-plug timeout). GOOD_BAT defaults low via an internal 950 kΩ pull-down; when low, the data switch remains open, and the dead-battery provision timer eventually disables charging. That creates an interface decision: if USB audio must work with no/weak battery while the auxiliary rail is powering the system, the design must define whether firmware may assert GOOD_BAT based on “system ready” rather than battery-good, or accept that SDP data stays disconnected. BQ24392 supports BC1.2 classes on USB-A-style D+/D−; its automatic detector has no disable input, and TI documents use with micro/mini USB, not concurrent Type-C/PD policy. Type-C current remains derived from CC/Rp or STUSB4500 contract; do not treat BC outputs as a Type-C/PD grant, and verify D+/D− coexistence with the configured Type-C/PD port before adopting it.

Next, preserve the MAX4995B as a candidate branch limiter, calculate the output diode's reverse-voltage requirement from worst-case protected-bus limits, and test battery-absent BQ startup and weak-source dropout. Measure the complete raw VBUS current waveform for at least 100 ms at ≥1 MS/s, use USBET's current algorithm to analyze every >100 mA region and its charge, and include reset/PD attach states. Keep the 9 V main path default-off until the bootstrap branch is isolated or safely rated.
